## What this is

The **PDAC Tissue Explorer** shows where a gene is expressed in human pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and how its expression differs between tumour and non-tumour pancreas, using three public datasets. It is meant for researchers and clinicians who want a quick, sample-level view without writing code. All figures and the underlying numbers can be downloaded.

## Data

| Source | What it contributes | Samples |
|---|---|---|
| **CPTAC PDAC** (Cao et al., *Cell* 2021), via LinkedOmics | Protein (TMT mass spectrometry, gene level), phosphosites and RNA in tumours and matched adjacent normal tissue; stage and overall survival | 135 PDAC tumours; 64 protein/phosphosite and 19 RNA tumour/normal pairs; ~50,000 phosphosites of ~7,800 genes; survival for 130 patients (72 deaths) |
| **TCGA-PAAD** and **GTEx** pancreas, uniformly processed by **recount3** (Wilks et al., *Genome Biol* 2021) | Bulk RNA of PDAC vs healthy pancreas | 147 PDAC (ductal histology), 4 adjacent normal, 330 healthy pancreas |
| **TCGA Pan-Cancer Clinical Data Resource** (Liu et al., *Cell* 2018) | Curated survival endpoints, stage and age of the TCGA-PAAD patients | 147 patients: 85 deaths, 93 progression events |
| **PDAC single-cell atlas** (Loveless et al., *Clin Cancer Res* 2025; 12 integrated studies) | Cell-type resolution: which cells express a gene, in which tissue state | ~620,000 cells from 234 samples: healthy donor, adjacent normal, primary tumour (treatment-naive and treated), metastases |

## Methods in brief

**CPTAC.** Protein values are log2 median-normalised intensities, RNA values are log2 RSEM upper-quartile, as provided by LinkedOmics. Adenosquamous carcinomas are excluded. Tumour and adjacent normal tissue are compared within the same patient (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test; exact for small samples); the median change has a 95% bootstrap confidence interval. Proteins of low abundance are often quantified in only part of the patients; the app reports this.

**CPTAC phosphosites.** Site-level phosphoproteome values (log2 median-normalised intensities from LinkedOmics), numbered on the RefSeq protein given with each site; sites listed under a former gene symbol are shown with the current gene. Values recorded as log2 of zero were treated as not quantified. A site is compared between tumour and matched adjacent normal tissue only when quantified in both tissues of at least 10 patients (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test, 95% bootstrap interval for the median change). Because a gene often has many sites, p-values are adjusted across the compared sites of that gene (Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate); a site is called higher or lower in tumour at FDR < 5%. *Relative to the protein level* subtracts the gene's protein level in the same sample before the comparison, so it shows changes in phosphorylation beyond changes in the amount of protein (available when the protein itself was quantified).

**Links to other resources.** The gene page links to NCBI Gene, UniProt, the Human Protein Atlas, Open Targets and DepMap, using the gene's Entrez or Ensembl identifier where it is unambiguous and a search for the symbol otherwise.

**Clinical context.** For the tumours of TCGA-PAAD (RNA) and CPTAC (protein and RNA), the app relates a gene's tumour level to survival, stage and tumour subtype. *Survival:* TCGA-PAAD overall survival and progression-free interval come from the TCGA Pan-Cancer Clinical Data Resource, which recommends both endpoints for pancreatic cancer; CPTAC overall survival comes from the study's follow-up (time to death or last contact). Kaplan–Meier curves compare tumours above the gene's median with those at or below it (log-rank test). Optionally, a data-driven cut-off is used instead: the split between the 20th and 80th percentile with the largest log-rank statistic (maximally selected rank statistics, Hothorn & Lausen 2003; R package maxstat). Searching for the best cut-off makes the ordinary log-rank p-value far too small (in our data, about 30% of genes with randomly shuffled survival reached p < 0.05), so the app reports only the p-value corrected for the search (Lausen & Schumacher 1992; 3% of shuffled genes below 0.05). The split, expressed as a percentile, is then applied to the other cohort (TCGA-PAAD to CPTAC RNA, CPTAC to TCGA-PAAD; overall survival), where no search takes place. A Cox model with a natural cubic spline (3 degrees of freedom) shows the hazard ratio across the range of values relative to a patient at the median, with tests for any association and for a departure from a straight line. Hazard ratios come from Cox proportional-hazards models of the continuous value, scaled to one standard deviation within the dataset, without adjustment and adjusted for age and stage (I, II, III–IV). *Stage:* Spearman correlation with pathological stage as an ordered variable (I < II < III < IV). *Classical vs basal-like:* per tumour, the mean z-score of the 25 basal-like genes minus the mean z-score of the 25 classical genes of Moffitt et al. (*Nat Genet* 2015), from the RNA of the same cohort (one classical gene, a non-coding locus, is not measured); higher scores are more basal-like. The app reports the Spearman correlation of the gene with this score and notes when the gene is itself one of the score's genes.

**Gene sets and survival.** Up to six genes, each with a chosen direction (high or low). Within each dataset, a gene is called high when above the median of the tumours with values for all selected genes (or in the top third) and low when at or below the median (or in the bottom third); calls are made before any clinical filter, so a patient's call does not change with the filters. Patients are grouped by the number of genes in their chosen direction (neighbouring counts merged until each group has at least 10 patients), by all genes vs fewer, or by a combined score (mean z-score, signed by the chosen direction; split at the median or at a data-driven cut-off as above). Cox models give the hazard ratio per additional gene, for all vs fewer, or per standard deviation of the score, unadjusted and adjusted for age and stage, in every dataset with the same filters; the same definition is shown in the other cohort. Filters: stage, sex, age and resection margin (CPTAC only).

**Co-expression within cell types.** For each cell type of the single-cell atlas, every treatment-naive primary tumour sample with at least 20 cells of that type contributes one value per gene: the average expression of the type in that sample (as in the cell-type view). Samples from studies contributing fewer than 3 such samples are left out, and each gene is centred within its study to remove study effects. Genes detected in at least 2% of the cells of the type in at least half of the samples are then compared with each other by Spearman's rank correlation of the centred values (degrees of freedom reduced by the number of studies). For each gene the app stores the 50 most positively and 20 most negatively correlated genes with their false discovery rate across all genes analysed in that cell type (Benjamini–Hochberg); 23 to 108 samples per cell type.

**TCGA/GTEx.** recount3 gene counts were converted to TPM and log2(TPM + 1). Only TCGA-PAAD primary tumours of ductal histology are used. Genes expressed at TPM ≥ 1 in at least 10% of samples of any cohort are included. TCGA and GTEx are different studies; no cross-study batch correction is applied, so differences are descriptive. Composition scores (fibroblast, endothelial, epithelial, acinar, immune) are mean log2 TPM of marker genes.

**Single-cell atlas.** The published atlas object was filtered before summarisation: the cycling-cell cluster was removed and cells with high ambient-RNA contamination (decontX score above 0.30, or above the 98th percentile within each sample and cell type) were excluded; cells with fewer than 200 detected genes were dropped. Expression values are the atlas's log-normalised values (log1p of counts per 10,000). For every sample and cell type the app stores the mean normalised expression and the fraction of cells with any detected transcript. *Average expression* = log1p(mean CP10k). A sample contributes to a cell type only with at least 20 cells of that type. Cell types are the atlas's annotations; ductal cells are split into those assigned to the atlas's tumour subclusters and the remaining (mostly non-malignant) ductal cells. Fibroblast subtypes (myFb 1–5, iFb 1–3) and tumour-cell groups (classical-like, hybrid, basal-like) follow the atlas publication. "Share of transcripts" is the fraction of all transcripts of a gene, pooled over all cells of a context, that come from each cell type. Tissue states: healthy donor; adjacent normal; treatment-naive primary tumour; treatment-naive liver metastasis; primary tumour after chemotherapy or chemoradiation.

## Limitations

- Abundance of an RNA or protein does not show its activity, modification or function. A change in a phosphosite's abundance does not by itself show a change in kinase activity or protein function, and phosphosites of low-abundance proteins are often quantified in few patients.
- Adjacent normal tissue from pancreatic-cancer surgery is inflamed and fibrotic; it is not healthy pancreas. Healthy pancreas (GTEx, donors) is mostly acinar tissue, so bulk tumour-vs-healthy differences partly reflect tissue composition.
- Single-cell RNA sequencing misses many low-abundance transcripts; "not detected" is not proof of absence.
- In the atlas, tissue state and treatment are partly confounded with the contributing study. Metastases are almost all liver metastases, and few metastatic samples contain enough fibroblasts.
- Survival, stage and subtype associations are observational and exploratory. Trying many genes, combinations, filters and cut-offs will produce some p-values below 0.05 by chance; agreement between the two independent cohorts is the more useful check. Survival after surgery depends on stage, resection margins, adjuvant therapy and tumour-cell content; the cohorts are small (about 130–150 patients) and follow-up is short, so estimates are imprecise, and among many genes some will reach p < 0.05 by chance. Bulk tumour values mix tumour and stromal cells, and the classical/basal-like score also tracks tumour-cell content.
- Co-expression across patients within a cell type can reflect shared regulation, a shared cell state or a varying mix of subtypes within the cell type; it does not show that two proteins interact or act in the same pathway. With 23–108 samples per cell type, weak correlations are not detected reliably. Transcripts typical of another cell type (e.g. tumour keratins detected in immune cells) can come from ambient RNA, and their co-expression then mostly tracks that contamination.
- p-values are unadjusted and refer to the single comparison stated (phosphosites: adjusted only across the sites of the selected gene); with many genes, some will be small by chance.
